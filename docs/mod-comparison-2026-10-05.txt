GenTown mod comparison — 5 October 2026

Current paultendo release: 1.6.93, commit 056c32d6b0ec367cad57725dcefad12c601011d5.
Upstream snapshot reviewed: 575f5f8a4bb4b07815ab348a7818485c403b9397.
All 15 root files downloaded at that pinned commit. The six native engine
scripts were fetched again from R74n and match the vendored bytes. SHA-256 hashes, results and
limits are in docs/qa/mod-comparison-2026-10-05.json. No other author's code was
copied into the mod. This audit made no gameplay changes or upstream PR.

What is worth learning from the other mods

1. better_mod_loader.mjs reports the exact failing mod and missing dependencies.
   This is a useful principle for our subsystems. Numerous catch blocks in our
   mod suppress errors, so a passing campaign cannot prove every feature worked.
   Use bounded, named diagnostics without flooding the Chronicle or stopping
   unrelated game systems. Do not adopt the experimental module loader itself.

2. world-configurator.js has an explicit redraw sequence after changing map
   dimensions: resize, terrain, highlight, cursor, statistics, composed canvas.
   Keep our fog and epidemic overlays in that lifecycle too. Our current import,
   refresh and next-day browser checks retained the map.

3. skip.js yields between turns and presents simple progress controls. Our
   autoplay already yields, pauses for genuine decisions and avoids answering
   unrelated confirmation dialogs. Keep deliberate player control. The skip mod
   attempts to click popup Yes/OK and default answers for any active prompt.

4. living-planet.js has a strong, consistent theme through its biome names and
   palette. This is a useful narrative lesson, not a reliability improvement.
   Our traveller, local makers and recorded consequences should supply that
   consistency without adding more independent narration systems.

Compatibility and rough edges found in the reviewed snapshot

- choices.js does not parse as JavaScript (Unexpected token ':').
- better-prompts.js imports a module. GenTown's standard loader inserts a classic
  script, which rejects that import. Its events/showPrompt targets also do not
  exist in the current native engine, which uses gameEvents/doPrompt. The custom
  loader is a separate integration requirement, not a drop-in improvement.
- Several mods use native Mod.afterLoad alone. Loading them after the game has
  started queues initialization until a later load. Our late-install regression
  passes, including the necessary map redraw.
- disasters.js ran 300 turns without uncaught errors but generated 25 events
  marked as needing input without a message or live controls. The precise event names are recorded.
- map-draw.js edits live chunks while the editor is open. Cancel hides the editor
  without restoring those changes. Its import validates width but not the whole
  map shape. It reports Save & Apply errors, which is a useful UX principle.
- 3d.js fetches external Three.js and controls scripts. Its animation frame loop
  continues to schedule while the feature is disabled. WebGL was not benchmarked.
- spacetravel.js holds additional worlds in a window cache. No save hooks for
  that cache were found in this file. Cross-world persistence needs a dedicated
  test before calling it safer. The campaign below did not complete a space trip.

No warnings in these runs. Our run closed 43 work records and crossed 28 real
technology thresholds. Its longest stretch without a tracked milestone was
12 turns, versus 17 in base. These measurements do not establish fun: different
mods change the random event stream and produce different populations. A tiny
work-status change counts as a milestone. Ordinary news does not.

Our 300-turn run logged 742 non-sunrise/sunset entries, versus 406 in base and
583 in News/Politics. More news is not necessarily better. Keep first discoveries,
changed obstacles and consequential outcomes prominent. Routine repetition
needs restraint so players can notice the rare, joyful or alarming events.

Later-game and reload checks

- Imported the actual seed-42 Yes campaign save from Day 601. All 59 visible
  restored story links opened a panel. No exceptions or warnings. The previously
  unresponsive crossing was not reproduced, so no speculative fix was made.
- Continued that save for 150 mixed-choice turns through Day 751. No missing
  controls, invariant failures, warnings or runtime errors. 42 additional work
  records reached an outcome. Two telescope projects remained waiting.
- These waits had a real dependency: missing glass and insufficient local heat
  technology to make it. The town needs hotter workshop methods or an actual
  supply route. They are not proved crashed jobs. Their persistent, generic
  'Gathering materials' status does not explain that chain well enough.
- Ran another 30 turns through Day 781 separately with CPU profiling. Passed.
  Median native nextDay call in jsdom: 345ms. P95: 505ms. Maximum: 629ms.
  Self-sample hotspots include save serialization, pathfinding and fog visibility.
  This is a profiling lead, not a measurement of browser frame rate or touch lag.
- 57 focused regressions passed for stories, workshop prerequisites, orders,
  glass and the campaign harness. Another 9 runtime tests passed for late loading,
  map/save restoration and autoplay-related cases. No full-suite rerun claimed.
- Real browser at an isolated origin: imported the save, opened the crossing,
  advanced to Day 602, refreshed, advanced to Day 603. Terrain remained visible.
  No captured console warnings or errors. User's existing game was untouched.
- 390x844 phone check: body width 390, Chronicle width and scroll width both 356.
  No horizontal clipping. Screenshots are in /private/tmp/gentown-comparison-*.png.

Release judgement

The reviewed mods do not provide convincing evidence of greater reliability.
Our current checks cover more recovery paths, but the mod carries much more
simulation and UI work. Before submitting the upstream PR, prioritise:

1. Show the actual chain behind blocked work, including knowledge, resources,
   committed supplies and available makers. Do not invent free completion.
2. Report caught subsystem failures with bounded diagnostics so a partial failure
   cannot quietly masquerade as a healthy game.
3. Profile actual browser turns on later saves and a slower device. Reduce repeated
   save, fog and route work before increasing speed or adding more simulation.
4. Run a short human playtest for quiet stretches, routine news volume and whether
   players understand what their choices changed. Automation cannot certify this.

Evidence is scoped to 1,680 campaign turns, including 480 on our mod. The 3D
renderer, every configuration, all policies and all possible mod combinations
were not tested. Extra protections are not a guarantee of crash-free play.

Follow-up: v1.6.94

The dependency views, named diagnostics, routine-update folding and
performance repairs prompted by this audit are now implemented. Decisions
use the main day control, with actual native answers in one focused panel.
A real browser turn also completed under fourfold CPU throttling. This
simulates slower processing, not a complete physical phone or network test.
The release checks and remaining pacing limits are in docs/release-1.6.94.txt.
The numbers and findings above retain their original v1.6.93 audit scope.
