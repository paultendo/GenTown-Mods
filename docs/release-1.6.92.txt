Campaign findings and repairs

Self-reporting events previously became invisible proposals and could later
crash the native independent-rule callback. They now run once. Real questions
keep their actual controls. A 1,000-day campaign also found that retired
secession and schism events entered the native random pool. Both initial
registration and catalog rebuilding now exclude them. Causal council and
religious movement systems remain active.

Population room now follows land, local methods and maintained buildings.
Recent meals and available room restrain growth. Migration carries actual
inhabitants, jobs, accepted goods and finite wealth. Crowding limits voluntary
moves without erasing conquered inhabitants. Jobs require an available person.

Famine follows sustained missed meals and recovery follows regular meals.
Native eating owns starvation deaths. A shipment cannot end famine before
people actually eat. Missing workshop ingredients create real upstream work
without reserving a waiting maker forever. Unknown glass cannot block fuel
needed by a separate inquiry. Costs, local knowledge and supply
constraints still apply. Workshop orders rotate among actual unmet needs
rather than favouring the first recipe indefinitely. This prevents recurring
farm-tool demand from starving a crew's boat request.

The final Seed 42 Yes campaign completed four crossings, with a fifth underway
on Day 601. Its earlier crew had remained unserved behind recurring tool orders.
The remaining telescopes in that campaign lack glass in towns whose local
fire knowledge is still 30. They cannot manufacture an unknown glass method.

Disposable route caches no longer serialize copied tiles into saves. Legacy
copies are removed on import. Real roads, traffic and histories remain saved.
The harness now catches occupations exceeding population, launcher autosave
failures and genuinely unchanged work. Repeated obstacles cannot fabricate
activity or hide stalled progress.

Limits and playtesting

These policies are repeatable probes, not models of a human player. The harsh
No campaign can lose all inhabitants. A passing run means it followed the
engine without the recorded errors, not that everyone survived or every job
finished. Food, fighting, missing supplies and unknown methods can legitimately
leave work waiting. All observed stalls and discovery timings are retained in
docs/qa/release-1.6.92.json and docs/qa/campaigns-1.6.92.txt.

Housing densities, response curves and other authored rules remain balance
decisions. Native daily turns are not seconds of human play. The campaigns
check causal consistency and progression but cannot prove enjoyable short,
medium and long term pacing. A player playtest remains the next acceptance
step. Old shared discoveries remain available where the save has no local
discovery provenance. The pass does not promise recovery of arbitrary corrupt
old saves or compatibility with other large overhaul mods.
