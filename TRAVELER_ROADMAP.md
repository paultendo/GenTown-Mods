# The Traveler: the next playable steps

The direction is a civilisation shaped by nudges, deeper exploration and interacting systems. The player should uncover stories, make uncertain choices and occasionally discover something delightful that nobody scripted for this particular run. The Traveler premise in GAME_CONCEPT.md remains the foundation. There is no correct route away from the end of the world.

## Choices become stories

The current implementation connects an answered discovery proposal to actual adoption and first production. Approved projects retain their own finished landmark. A selectable choice chapter shows the proposal, response, immediate changes and what followed. Law, research, healthcare and diplomatic choices can also show current conditions separately from the original outcome. Refused advice stays refused. Old saves gain no invented decisions or dates.

This is the first step toward visible consequences, not a complete causal model. A later event belongs to a choice only when the simulation supplies an explicit link. Changes in population, wealth or conflict alone cannot prove that the player caused them.

## Exploration reveals a place worth knowing

Species now have a discoverable history: new encounters remember the resident’s town and date, habitat and traits appear in naming prompts and species pages, and native cultivation, breeding and town-animal adoption leave dated notes. Settlement and species pages link to each other. Historical facts are not invented for old saves. The engine aggregates crop yields, so these pages do not claim particular harvest quantities for particular species.

Completed expeditions now retain a named destination, the actual arrival and journey date, known origin, person carrying a whisper, and the terrain revealed. The settlement and temporary expedition marker lead to the place. The page shows current terrain, actual roads and later ownership changes, and lets the player ask a person to return. A rival’s claim can become hostile words aimed at that specific owner. These words can worsen relations and war pressure, but cannot silently target somebody else if the ground changes hands. Merely considering an expedition creates no historical visit. Fog and unknown identities remain protected.

The seeded first inheritance now supplies three finite artifacts. Placement records the actual action without inventing an earlier expedition. Finders come from actual workers and figures, and knowledge and outlook determine whether an object becomes a curiosity, a healing tool, a relic, a shared tune, a subject of study or an instrument of military discipline. A whisper can encourage sharing or secrecy. Sharing can pass an object to a worker with useful knowledge even if the player has never opened their profile. Occupation, the end of a settlement and rediscovery can change custody without discarding its history. Merely visiting another world does not replenish an object.

Eight artifact tests cover finite placement, private randomness, actual paths, prerequisite-dependent use, different finders, actual influence changes, reloads, sharing and withheld knowledge, actual occupation and rediscovery, guarded refusal, fog and travel to another world. Artifacts on inactive worlds do not yet develop their histories while the player is elsewhere.

Species proof: native encounter, naming, cultivation and town-animal callbacks are exercised with reload and visibility checks. The existing three seeded 300-day simulations also record encounters during normal play. Six destination tests cover native acceptance, refusal and failed paths, marker expiry, persistent visits, saved return whispers and actual traffic, known lost origins, visibility, actual ownership changes and a delayed hostile claim. The three seeded 300-day runs exercise the existing expedition systems alongside normal play.

## People carry the consequences

Known settlements now offer named contacts drawn from their actual jobs and established figures. A direct whisper is saved as words still carried by that person. Two days later, their outlook, individual and community trust, local values and current pressures determine reception. A worker may turn teaching into their own practice, a farmer may survey for resources and a priest may lead a pilgrimage. A surplus town can send actual food to a hungry known trade partner along a real path. Good intentions can become military discipline. Darker suggestions can undermine obedience or be interpreted as a reason for tighter control. Claims against reachable neighbours reduce relations and add persistent war pressure, with actual conflict using the existing war simulation. Generous recipients are less willing to treat neighbours as prey.

These contacts are a small window into the population, not a simulated life for every resident. They are first met through the settlement page and retain their identity, outlook, trust and whisper history across reloads. Established figures keep their existing deeds and mortality. Workers who leave a job cannot act as though they still hold it. Advice has personal and global intervals, and the world does not pause for these words. Caravans now carry practices between communities, with local listeners changing their meaning. Forgotten memories, succession and a complete social network remain future work.

Proof: ten tests exercise comparable words through different people and trust, changing conditions, reloads, real resource conservation and routes, exploration and fog, deceased figures, dissent versus suppression, hostile relations, war pressure and the native war process. Removing the route changes a food gift into local care. Unknown towns cannot supply whisper targets.

## The world surprises us

Connect ecology, production, routes, settlement needs and local memory into small chains. A useful species can change what people grow. Repeated exchange can sustain a route. Contact can spread a practice and change the towns at either end. Joy can be a first shared harvest, an unexpected friendship, a landmark finally used or a local story becoming a tradition. Celebrate the actual moment briefly and leave a trace in the place where it happened.

Proof: multiple seeds yield different chains through normal play. Removing an enabling condition changes the outcome. There is no timer that guarantees a happy vignette or a hidden verdict on the player's theory.

## The Traveler frame reaches the game

The fresh-world opening now introduces the fragmented memory and uncertain role beside the Chronicle without blocking the first camp. Lore retains that memory and leads to known people. Ongoing saves receive no fabricated arrival event. Artifacts now connect remembered places to actual workers and figures. Visions remain to be built. Preserve ongoing saves and familiar controls while that framing develops. The game should gradually make the player feel like a visitor influencing history rather than an administrator confirming every popup.

Proof: the opening establishes the role in natural game language, interventions have limits and reception, and the world can continue without turning unanswered proposals into silent approval.

## Objects return through time

Run zero begins with a small seeded inheritance. Later runs should inherit objects that actually existed in previous play, including things civilisation made. Their makers, custodians, uses, damage and legends should travel with them. There should be no guaranteed replacement for something lost or destroyed, and inheritance should require a route back into the Traveler’s hands. A useful instrument can eventually become the very object brought to an earlier beginning. The player discovers this loop through familiar details rather than a catalogue of bonuses.

The loop is now playable. Your suggestions can lead a real worker to attempt an independent object or a rough successor to an inherited one. Required knowledge, materials, the worker and access to the reference must still be present at completion. Actual resources are spent, and the maker and any predecessor remain part of the object’s identity. The player can give the finished work a name. Its rough construction reduces the strength of its eventual use.

A bearer can refuse the request or make a promise after two days, with the handover following later. Use, outlook, whether the maker already knows this work and actual working substitutes shape the wait. A healer can keep a needed lens through illness. Fighting, work using the object and a full pack can delay delivery. A changed bearer cancels the old promise. Asking them to hurry remains subject to consent and costs real trust, with influence changes tied to the object’s use. Returning through Lore begins the same terrain again with the pack currently in the Traveler’s hands, including work made during play. No missing object is regenerated. The pack holds five objects. Objects retain names, makers, predecessor identity and dated histories. Stories of things left behind remain in Other histories. The complete last world can be revisited after reload. Older returns keep object stories without recursively storing every complete save. Native Start new planet remains a separate fresh game.

Thirteen loop tests cover material-backed creation and naming, missing prerequisites and interrupted work, independent player ideas, predecessor identity, actual return to day one and saved inheritance, refusal and empty hands, reloads, revisiting the last world, histories across multiple returns, saved promises, illness and replacements, purpose-dependent timing, an early handover’s costs, changed custody, pack capacity, work progress and repeated practice. The browser check names a completed successor, obtains it from its actual bearer and reads its prior history after the return.

The next depth is autonomous craft, transmission of practices between settlements, more kinds of objects and visions that connect possible futures to these histories. The current crafts are three kinds of instrument with individual identities, not a general simulation of every possible manufactured object.

## Time follows the story

The first playable balance separates response, development and legacy. Words receive an answer after two days. Base crafting times range from six to ten days depending on the kind of object, with visible work partway through. Better smithing and previous successful attempts shorten later work. A promise to return an object is a separate process, shaped by its purpose, unfamiliarity, personal attachment and whether another usable object can do the work. Its earliest handover is recalculated from those conditions without a daily reroll or repeatedly restarting a countdown. A genuinely new purpose begins another period of use.

These small episodes should overlap with exploration, proposals and town life. A successful object can become a later replacement, shorten another promise, interrupt care when taken too soon, change hands in conflict, or return in a later beginning. The world does not suspend itself while a whisper settles. Progress reports and dated links make the connection visible without promising a reward for every suggestion.

The long rhythm remains the weakest layer. Custody, inherited histories and caravan transmission now persist. Actual harvests, shortages and academy work can begin practices without the Traveler. Autonomous invention, deeper traditions and visions remain future work. Fixed days are calibration values, not proof of enjoyable pacing. Human playtests should record time to first meaningful reply, gaps with no useful choices or visible development, overlapping unfinished stories, and whether players remember a consequence when it returns. Tune those gaps and connections, rather than simply adding more random events or stretching every timer.

## Needs and beliefs connect the existing mechanics

Technology, diplomacy, war, faith and culture remain the substance of the world. The Traveler provides a viewpoint. The common foundation should be needs, wants, means, relationships and memories that produce behaviour without a player prompt.

Food, health, safety and access come from actual stores, disease, conflict and reachable routes. Status, control, belonging, curiosity and spiritual meaning create further ambitions even when immediate survival is secure. Meeting a need can change what a community wants next. Use existing town values, influences, jobs, institutions, figures and local memories as inputs before inventing more meters.

A need must leave several plausible responses, limited by what the town knows and can actually do. Hunger might lead to exchange, sharing, migration, protest or predation. Current routes, neighbours, leaders, customs and remembered treatment shape that choice. A wealthy town can pursue a rival's prestige or defend its faith. Material deprivation must not mechanically imply malice, and spiritual life must not be reduced to hunger with a different label.

Public explanations and underlying pressures can differ. A ruler may call expansion sacred while seeking security and prestige. Residents may repeat that account, reinterpret it or doubt it. Private motives should affect choices where modelled. The narrator reports behaviour and attributes claims to their speakers, rather than claiming certainty about a subconscious motive. No need for a full psychological simulation of every resident. Begin with communities and the named contacts whose actions already matter.

First playable slice: actual food pressure, remembered exchanges and existing governance choose between help, exchange and hostile action along real paths. Each action needs an eligible actor, actual costs, changed relationships, a remembered source and follow-ups at different times. Shortage recovery should end the pressure without erasing the memory. Test conservation, blocked roads, competing responses, independent autonomy and histories across reloads before adding more domains.

Chronicle groundwork in v1.6.43 links existing decisions, first harvests, object custody/work, whispers and teachings to recorded histories. It removes the old narrator's keyword-based causal guesses. This is history infrastructure, not the completed wants-and-needs model.

## Commodities connect needs to the world

Food is a commodity within the economy. Build the common foundation around goods, ownership, uses, production, transformation, storage, consumption and exchange. A shortage is one pressure on that system. Avoid a separate scripted response machine for every resource.

Demand comes from possible uses. Grain feeds residents, supports an expedition, fills a reserve or becomes an offering. Lumber can finish a school or build ships. Rock and metal support construction and craft. Using a supply for one purpose can prevent another. Material claims should come from actual ongoing work, available knowledge, accessible resources and consumption, rather than a fixed shopping list or a generic influence bonus standing in for a shipment.

Value belongs to a particular community in a particular situation. Urgency, substitutes, expected replenishment, access, rival buyers, transport, remembered obligations and acceptable alternatives affect an offer. Currency is one means of exchange. Barter and aid can exist before it. Prices and quantities must have state-dependent inputs, while tuning constants remain explicit calibration choices, not proof of economic realism.

Beliefs shape acceptable uses and terms. Shared provision, private ownership, reciprocal obligation, protection of one's own people, religious offerings and prestige can coexist or conflict. A community can honour an earlier gift while refusing a stranger. A merchant may prefer an exchange even when a neighbour would give freely. People and institutions within a town need not agree. Public claims are attributable speech, not the narrator's privileged access to everyone's motives.

Repeated exchange can create dependence, friendships, obligations and leverage. Open borders should grant actual access to people, routes and places. Betrayal needs that access, an actor, a desired outcome, capacity, a risky action and consequences that the other town can discover. Permission alone must never teleport ownership across a border. Trade, diplomacy and war should act upon the same stores, routes and claims.

Unique objects also have owners and possible uses, but are not interchangeable units. The Traveler's instruments carry identities, provenance, attachments and histories. Sacred or irreplaceable things may be withheld regardless of an attractive material offer. A future general economy must preserve those differences.

In v1.6.44, a shared foundation connects saved exchange, cargo, payment, terms and history across the five native stocks: grain, livestock, stone, timber and metal. Actual meals and unfinished construction or craft create demand. Construction substitutes and craft reservations compete for the same stores. Caravans and market purchases now use that foundation instead of inventing cargo, earnings or influence bonuses. Imported materials record their actual use in meals, buildings and created objects. Public spending and taxes move actual divisible cash between public stores and native private wealth. This foundation has logic tests and browser checks, but is not a complete commodity economy. Other production chains, local knowledge of offers, competing institutions, appropriation, border betrayal and longer-term dependencies remain to be implemented.

## Materials and knowledge develop together

Commodity demand must evolve through usable techniques and competing purposes. Technology unlocks possible uses, not a universal price increase or an automatic appetite for every newly available resource. Demand then comes from people actually choosing or needing those uses. A school creates a construction claim. An instrument creates a craft claim. A better substitute can reduce an older material's importance in one use while it remains essential elsewhere.

Encounter, recognition, access and practical knowledge are distinct. A town can encounter an unfamiliar material through exploration, mining, a gift or a shipment before it can work it. An actual sample, someone willing and able to investigate, facilities and time can support an experiment. Its outcome may reveal a use, fail, injure someone, provoke objections or be withheld. Successful practical knowledge can travel with people and exchange. Ownership of a deposit, knowledge of a technique and a supply ready to use must not be interchangeable flags.

New commodities require physical sources or transformations. Clay can be encountered in suitable ground and gain new uses with firing. Charcoal requires wood, controlled fire and work. Glass requires suitable inputs and a usable furnace. An alloy requires its actual constituent materials and a process. Coal may be found before it becomes an important fuel, while improved mining later makes deposits reachable. These are proposed chains, not currently simulated stocks. Add each with geography or inputs, actual outputs, a native visual description, uses, substitutes and meaningful constraints. Do not distribute invented stock to every town when a technology milestone is reached.

An unfamiliar material can motivate research, but receiving a sample must not award instant technology or profitable demand. Curiosity, current problems, institutions, beliefs, safety, alternatives and remembered results determine whether experimentation continues. Knowledge changes valuation only through actual opportunities or plausible beliefs attributed to the people holding them. A trader can speculate and be wrong. A priest can value a material for ritual while a smith sees a practical use. A community can understand a technique and still refuse it.

The current engine's technology milestones remain global. The existing uses respect those milestones and actual actors, but it does not yet give each settlement its own material knowledge or a general experimental production system. Those are the next connected layer, alongside autonomous invention, rather than another set of isolated unlock events.

In v1.6.45, clay, charcoal and fired bricks form the first material chain. Successful mining on owned wetland exchanges one unit of native stone yield for a finite clay sample. The wetland source, forty-unit pockets, recipe quantities, work durations and first-trial risks are explicit game calibration, not geological or industrial measurements. Charcoal consumes two timber and takes three work days. A brick firing consumes two clay and one charcoal and takes five work days. Actual miners or lumberers begin trials through curiosity or unfinished construction. First trials can fail. Hunger, war and lost workers pause work, and saved trials keep their original roll. Completed work teaches only its own settlement. Shared global techniques enable work and lower firing risk, while recent actual brick firings raise the existing kiln proposal's research weight. Global milestones still belong to the native technology model.

Bricks substitute for stone in actual construction. Pending workshop inputs share the common claims, barter, cargo and use history. Construction, fuel and other craft can compete for the same timber. A received material is encountered locally but does not teach its manufacturing process. Town and Chronicle links expose the actual maker, inputs, failed or completed firings and current work. These mechanisms also run beyond the player's explored land without exposing hidden identities. Glass, alloys, coal mining, recipe teaching by traveling people, pottery storage, dedicated workshop buildings and competing institutions remain future work.
